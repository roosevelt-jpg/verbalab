# Lugemi
## Brand guidelines
Version 1.1 | 5 October 2026 | Proposed standards for founder review

Global language intelligence. Africa first.

This guide defines the proposed Lugemi identity for a language intelligence platform prioritizing African languages and serving culturally distinct global markets. Public site: https://lugemi.com. It defines brand standards, not a request to modify the application. The name and visual direction come from the founder's current exploration. Positioning, exact colours, typography, product names and implementation standards below are recommendations, not claims of prior approval. Generated logo images are concept references; production vector masters are still required. Domain ownership, trademark clearance, legal entity, supported languages, feature availability and commercial promises remain unverified. No invented contact details are provided.

## 01 | Brand at a glance
### The essential identity
Brand name: Lugemi. Proposed pronunciation: loo-GEH-mee. Category: first-party language intelligence infrastructure (our API and our models). Strategic focus: Africa first, with Latin America, Southeast Asia, the Middle East, the EU, the Caribbean, Russia, Ukraine, India and Pakistan in the strategic scope. Personality: welcoming, capable, precise and culturally respectful. Core visual identity: a teal speech bubble containing a rounded L, paired with a navy rounded wordmark.
### The operating principle
Make language technology useful in the languages people actually speak. Begin with evaluated language and dialect coverage, understandable workflows and clear controls. Let actual product capability determine promises. Africa-first is the product development priority; global usefulness is part of the brand scope. Distinguish current task availability from planned geographic and language coverage.
### Quick implementation rules
Use Lugemi in title case. Use the existing wordmark artwork rather than typing a substitute. Use navy for primary text, teal for recognition and deep teal for accessible interactive controls. Use Noto Sans for interface content with script-specific fallbacks. Publish language availability by capability. Keep synthetic-voice status visible where relevant. Use the latest corrected logo concepts as references; exclude damaged transparency exports and glow effects.

## 02 | Purpose, mission and vision
### Purpose
Help people communicate, create and access knowledge through their own languages. Lugemi should remove avoidable language barriers while respecting the people whose speech and knowledge make the technology possible.
### Mission
Build practical language intelligence tools that prioritize African languages, dialects and accents, while enabling people and organizations across global markets to work through speech, text and digital content with their own linguistic and cultural context.
### Vision
A future in which language does not determine who can participate in digital services, business, education or creative work. This is an aspiration, not a claim that Lugemi already serves every language.
### Values expressed through behaviour
Inclusion: assess underserved language needs before copying market defaults. Respect: preserve names, orthography, tone marks and local context. Clarity: explain outputs, cost and limitations in plain language. Reliability: communicate measured quality and recoverable failures. Agency: give people meaningful controls over recordings, voices and data. Partnership: credit and compensate contributors under documented agreements.

## 03 | Positioning and audience
### Positioning statement
For creators, developers and organizations working across African and global languages, Lugemi is a language intelligence platform designed to make speech and text workflows accessible through one coherent experience. Its intended distinction is Africa-first investment, local language depth, dialect awareness and useful integration, demonstrated through evaluated capability rather than broad coverage claims.
### Public product category
Lugemi is first-party language intelligence infrastructure: our API and our models (speech, text, translation). The comparable category is a first-party language/speech platform (first-party speech platforms), not a wrapper around Google Translate, OpenAI . Strategic regions besides Africa include Latin America, Southeast Asia, the Middle East and the EU; do not claim every language is live. Domain: https://lugemi.com. Packages, env vars, and headers use Lugemi (`@lugemi/*`, `LUGEMI_*`, `X-Lugemi-*`). See `docs/brand/PUBLIC_POSITIONING.md`.
### Priority audiences
Creators and media teams need narration, transcription, dubbing and subtitles with controllable quality. Developers need predictable APIs, clear documentation and capability-specific language metadata. Businesses need understandable translation and communication workflows. Research and language partners need transparent evaluation, attribution and contribution terms. Education and public-service teams need accessible content and review workflows; sector suitability must be validated separately.
### Messaging priorities
Creators: sound natural and keep control. Developers: integrate clearly and measure quality. Organizations: communicate across languages with review and governance. Language communities: preserve linguistic detail and participate on clear terms. Avoid claiming clinical, legal or government suitability merely because a sector appears in marketing.

## 04 | Brand story and differentiation
### Founder-ready narrative
People should not need to abandon their language to benefit from technology. Lugemi begins with African languages because their diversity deserves deliberate product investment. Our ambition is to turn speech, text and knowledge into useful tools for people, creators and organizations, with local context and clear controls built into the experience. Africa is our starting priority; communities across Latin America, Southeast Asia, the Middle East, the EU, the Caribbean, Russia, Ukraine, India and Pakistan belong in our global vision.
### Evidence that makes the story credible
Publish evaluated language coverage, dialect-specific examples, representative audio samples, documented review workflows and integration examples. Attribute collaborators with permission. Show how the platform handles difficult names, code-switching and regional accents. Separate publicly released capability from experiments and roadmap items.
### Competitive expression
Use category-familiar workflows where they help users understand the product, while developing Lugemi's own visual language, navigation and copy. Do not imitate another provider's logo, proprietary interface, illustrations or branded feature names. Comparison claims require dated, reproducible evidence. Prefer a concrete workflow demonstration over unsupported statements such as best, first, perfect or human-indistinguishable.

## 05 | Name and verbal identity
### Name rules
Write Lugemi in ordinary prose, headings and navigation. LUGEMI is reserved for small editorial labels where uppercase is deliberate. Do not write LuGemi, LugEMI or Lu Gemi. The name is coined: it has no verified literal translation or established acronym. The proposed pronunciation is a brand convention, not an African-language etymology.
### Meaning and tagline
Proposed brand meaning: language that connects people. Primary tagline: Global language intelligence. Africa first. Use this as an ambition-led brand line. Supporting line: Language intelligence, built around you. Product descriptor: The Africa-first language intelligence platform for global markets. Do not convert the tagline into an assertion of current universal language support.
### Naming architecture
Use Lugemi as the master brand. Proposed labels include Lugemi Studio for the workspace, Lugemi Voice for speech tools, Lugemi Translate for translation, Lugemi Transcribe for transcription, Lugemi Dubbing for localization, Lugemi API for developers and Lugemi Enterprise for organizational offerings. Use only names matching actual released features. Keep internal model names separate from customer-facing task labels.

## 06 | Logo system and asset status
### The mark
The speech bubble represents communication. Its rounded L creates a direct link to Lugemi. Rounded typography reinforces approachability. This explanation is an intended design meaning, not an established cultural symbol. Keep the bubble shape, L, lettering and spacing together as a coherent system.
### Logo family
Primary horizontal: teal mark and navy wordmark on white or a light neutral. Reversed colour: teal mark and white wordmark on navy. Monochrome black: black artwork on white. Monochrome white: white artwork on dark, quiet backgrounds. Stacked: centred mark above the wordmark. Symbol only: app icon, favicon, compact navigation or an already identified brand context.
### Asset quality requirement
The images generated in this conversation are raster concepts with small variation between exports. They are not interchangeable production masters. Some transparent variants contain rough edges or halos and must not be shipped. Reconstruct one approved master as vector paths, derive every variant from it and compare proportions before release. The corrected opaque previews establish the preferred colour and layout direction. This guide does not include final SVG or EPS artwork.

## 07 | Logo spacing, scale and placement
### Clear space
Define H as the height of the speech-bubble body, excluding its tail. Reserve at least 0.25H clear space on all sides of the full logo. For the symbol alone, use the same minimum around the symbol. No text, border, partner mark or image detail may enter this zone. Once the vector master is drawn, lock the icon-to-wordmark spacing from that master rather than adjusting each export.
### Minimum sizes
Proposed minimum horizontal width: 144 CSS px on screen and 30 mm in print. Stacked width: 112 CSS px or 24 mm. Standalone symbol: 24 CSS px or 6 mm. A 16 px favicon requires a separately checked optical version. Increase these sizes if the L, counters or lettering lose clarity. CSS display size and export pixel dimensions are separate.
### Placement
Website and console: place the horizontal logo at the top-left within normal page padding. Social avatar: use the symbol centred with sufficient crop clearance. Documents: use a quiet header or cover area. Avoid repeating a large logo on every slide. App-store icons require a dedicated square export checked against the store's current specifications. Do not embed marketing text in the app icon.

## 08 | Logo integrity and partnerships
### Prohibited alterations
Do not stretch, rotate, skew, retype, outline or rearrange the wordmark. Do not change the bubble's tail, replace the L, add a flag, apply textures or place the logo inside a decorative badge. Do not add glows, bevels or shadows. Do not recolour it to match a campaign without brand-owner approval. Avoid detailed photographs directly behind the mark; use a solid panel or a quiet area.
### Background selection
Use navy artwork on light surfaces and white artwork on dark surfaces. Keep the teal mark on approved light or navy backgrounds. On ambiguous mid-tone surfaces, use a solid backing panel. Although logotypes are exempt from the WCAG text contrast requirement, Lugemi's house rule is to keep them clearly readable.
### Partner lockups
Separate logos with a neutral divider and at least each logo's own clear space. Match visual prominence rather than raw width. Use relationship labels such as integrated with only when accurate. Obtain permission for partner artwork and approved usage. Never imply endorsement, certification or sponsorship from an integration alone. Do not add the registered-trademark symbol without verified registration.

## 10 | Functional colours and contrast
### Semantic palette
Success: #16794A. Warning text: #8A4B00 on #FFF4DD. Error: #B42318. Information: #2457A7. Pair states with clear labels and icons, never colour alone. Keep status colours distinct from the brand teal. Disabled controls need readable explanations even where contrast exceptions apply.

| White #FFFFFF | Deep teal #007C78 | Approved |

### Accessibility standard
Target WCAG 2.2 AA across the actual product. Normal text requires at least 4.5:1 contrast; qualifying large text requires 3:1. Important control boundaries and graphical cues generally require 3:1 under the applicable non-text criterion. This guide does not establish product conformance. Brand teal is decorative on white; use deep teal for functional elements. Test opacity, hover states, photos and actual rendered combinations. Sources: W3C WCAG 2.2 and Understanding Contrast Minimum; links are in section 27.

## 11 | Typography
### Brand and interface type
Proposed primary family: Noto Sans, weights 400, 500, 600 and 700. Use it for website headings, interface text, documentation and marketing body copy. The custom rounded logo lettering is artwork, not a font recommendation. Do not rebuild the wordmark using Noto Sans. Proposed code family: Noto Sans Mono, with an appropriate system monospace fallback.
### Type scale
| Role | Desktop size / line height | Weight |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hero | 48 / 56 px | 700 |
| Page heading | 36 / 44 px | 700 |
| Section heading | 28 / 36 px | 600 |
| Card heading | 20 / 28 px | 600 |
| Body | 16 / 24 px | 400 |
| Compact interface | 14 / 20 px | 400 or 500 |
| Caption | 12 / 18 px | 400 |
### Layout rules
Reduce hero text to 32/40 px on small screens. Prefer sentence case and left-aligned body copy, reversing alignment where the script requires it. Use 45-75 characters per body line where appropriate to the script. Avoid forced uppercase in translations and tight letter spacing on connected scripts. Use tabular numerals for changing numerical data. PDF typography may use an embedded substitute where brand fonts are unavailable; that does not change the product standard.

## 12 | Multilingual typography and localization
### Script-aware implementation
Noto is a collection of script-specific families, not one font file covering every writing system. Load the required families after confirming language/script needs. Candidate families include Noto Sans for supported Latin and Cyrillic content, Noto Sans Devanagari, Noto Sans Bengali, Noto Sans Tamil, Noto Sans Telugu, Noto Sans Thai, Noto Sans Khmer, Noto Sans Myanmar, Noto Nastaliq Urdu, Noto Sans Ethiopic, Noto Sans Arabic, Noto Sans Adlam, Noto Sans NKo and Noto Sans Tifinagh. Confirm each family against real test strings, licences and shaping behaviour before release. Script and language are separate metadata fields.
### Respectful language presentation
Retain diacritics and tone marks, including in names, exports, search and subtitles. Do not use a country's flag as a language selector. Show local language names alongside an interface-language label when useful. Store language, dialect, script and locale explicitly; use valid BCP 47 tags where defined and separate structured metadata where needed. Never guess a dialect code.
### Review and internationalization
Use UTF-8 end-to-end. Preserve original text alongside any normalized search index. Support right-to-left layout and mixed-direction segments without reversing numbers, code or product names incorrectly. Test combining marks, selection, copy/paste, wrapping and subtitles. Allow translated copy to expand; do not hardcode card heights. Have fluent reviewers assess cultural fit, spelling and pronunciation. Maintain a glossary and record the locale and reviewer for each approved translation.

## 13 | Voice and tone
### Voice attributes
Welcoming: invite users into a task without making assumptions about expertise. Capable: explain the next action confidently. Precise: name the language, task and limitation. Respectful: treat language communities as partners and audiences, not raw data sources. Keep sentences direct and avoid exaggerated AI claims.
### Tone by situation
Onboarding: friendly and instructional. Technical documentation: concise, literal and reproducible. Billing: transparent about charges and timing. Errors: calm, actionable and specific. Language contribution: appreciative, clear about rights and compensation. Sensitive communication: restrained and review-oriented. Marketing: optimistic, grounded in available capability.
### Examples
Prefer: Choose a language to start your narration. Avoid: Unlock limitless linguistic magic. Prefer: This voice is in preview. Pronunciation may vary. Avoid: Perfect speech in every dialect. Prefer: We could not process this recording. Try a shorter file or check its format. Avoid: Oops! Something went wrong. Prefer: Review the transcript before publishing. Avoid: Your transcript is guaranteed accurate.

Short description: Lugemi is an Africa-first language intelligence platform for speech, text and cross-language workflows across global markets. Expanded description: Lugemi is being built to help creators, developers and organizations work across African and global languages through a coherent language intelligence workspace. Its focus is useful workflows, linguistic detail and clear controls. Replace being built only when product availability justifies it.
### Website starter copy
Hero: Global language intelligence. Africa first. Supporting copy: Create, understand and communicate through speech, translation and language tools built around local context, with African languages at the centre of our investment. Primary call to action: Explore Lugemi. Secondary: View language coverage. Capability cards may use Generate speech, Transcribe audio and Translate text only when those functions are available.
### Editorial rules
Use sentence case, active voice and concrete verbs. Spell out unfamiliar acronyms on first use. Write AI-generated with the hyphen where grammatical. Use language names consistently from the approved glossary. Avoid native-quality, all African languages, zero errors and real-time unless release evidence supports the exact claim. Specify currencies, billing periods, usage units and applicable limitations. Domain names and contact addresses must come from verified business records, not this guide.

## 15 | Product architecture and language coverage
### Task-first navigation
Group experiences by user goals: Create speech, Transcribe, Translate, Dub content, Manage voices and Build with APIs. Add categories only when product scope warrants them. Use one master brand across the console; avoid turning every tool into a separate logo or colour identity.
### Capability matrix
For each language or dialect, publish separate availability for transcription, translation direction, speech generation, dubbing and other released features. Use Available, Preview, Research and Planned as distinct states. Planned is never selectable as a released capability. Preview descriptions must state practical limitations. Do not infer translation coverage from TTS coverage.
### Quality and model transparency
Show supported scripts, relevant regional variety, model version, evaluation date and example conditions where feasible. Display confidence only if calibrated for the task and population. Do not use arbitrary confidence percentages as decoration. Keep model names in advanced settings and documentation rather than primary navigation. Mark generated samples as synthetic and identify sample language/variety. A demo must use functioning outputs or clearly state that it is illustrative.

## 16 | Interface layout and components
### Layout system
Use an 8 px spacing system with a 4 px sub-step for compact details. Suggested tokens: 4, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32, 48 and 64 px. Main content max-width: 1200 px on marketing pages; task workspaces may expand for timelines and editors. Desktop grids: 12 columns; tablet: 8; mobile: 4. Typical gutters: 24 px desktop, 16 px mobile. These are implementation defaults, not constraints against accessible reflow.
### Component rules
Use 12 px card radius, 8 px button/input radius and pill radius only for compact status tags. Primary buttons use deep teal with white text. Secondary buttons use navy text and a clear border. Every interactive component needs hover, focus, active, loading and disabled states. Use visible field labels. Keep one primary action per focused task area.
### Accessibility and responsive behaviour
Provide keyboard access, visible focus and meaningful screen-reader names. Set a house target size of at least 44 by 44 CSS px for primary controls. Allow 200% zoom and narrow viewport reflow without losing tasks. Do not rely on placeholder text as a label. Treat destructive actions with explicit descriptions and a recoverable path where possible. Never use a decorative border as the only required control boundary if contrast is insufficient.

## 17 | Dark mode and data visualization
### Dark-mode roles
Canvas: #0B1426. Surface: #12213A. Raised surface: #192C49. Primary text: #F7FAFC. Secondary text: #B6C3D5. Functional accent: brand teal #00B8AE with navy text on filled controls. Validate each actual state; dark mode is a separate semantic theme rather than an inverted screenshot. Retain deep teal where its pairing is appropriate, not by automatic substitution.
### Charts and audio editors
Use labels, shapes, patterns or line styles in addition to colour. Prefer direct series labels to distant legends. Use units and denominators, dates, sample sizes and explicit unknown states. Do not truncate axes to exaggerate improvement. Waveforms should represent actual audio when used as a functional editor. Decorative waveforms must not imply measured audio data.
### Trust indicators
Differentiate recording, processing, ready and failed with text and icons. A pulsing dot may supplement recording status but cannot replace it. Keep cost, duration, selected language and model visible near the generation action. Mark estimates as estimates. Never create fictional performance charts, customer totals or compliance badges for visual polish.

## 18 | Imagery, illustration and iconography
### Photography
Show contemporary people in credible language-use settings: studios, offices, classrooms and everyday communication. Represent regional, age and gender diversity across the priority markets, without treating Africa or any other region as one visual culture. Obtain appropriate rights and consent. Do not imply a photographed person is a customer, contributor or voice owner without permission. Disclose synthetic imagery when its presentation would otherwise imply a real person or event.
### Illustration
Use simple rounded geometry, restrained teal accents and generous whitespace. Speech, connection and layered content are useful themes. Avoid generic tribal patterns, exoticized imagery or decorative scripts used without understanding. Cultural motifs require a specific context and review. Never make a language or script ornamental at the expense of readability.
### Icons
Use one coherent outline icon family at 24 px with approximately 2 px stroke, adjusted consistently across the set. Filled icons can indicate selection when the family supports them. Do not mix arbitrary styles. Pair unfamiliar icons with labels. Microphones can identify recording actions; speech bubbles can identify messaging. The Lugemi symbol remains a brand mark rather than a generic button icon.

## 19 | Motion and sonic expression
### Motion principles
Use animation to explain change, processing and continuity. Suggested durations: 120-160 ms for small feedback, 180-240 ms for panels and 280-360 ms for page transitions. Use gentle ease-out for entry and short ease-in for exit. Respect prefers-reduced-motion; replace movement with immediate state changes or restrained fades. Avoid continuous animation in long reading and editing tasks.
### Audio behaviour
Do not autoplay generated speech, sound effects or a sonic logo. Offer clear play, pause, stop and volume controls. Recording status must be both visible and accessible. Audio previews should identify language, variety, voice and synthetic status as relevant. Provide transcripts or captions for informational audio/video.
### Optional sonic identity
A brief two-note sound can become a future recognition cue, but no sonic asset has been commissioned or approved. Avoid appropriating ceremonial sounds or stereotypical African percussion as a default identity. Any commissioned sound requires rights clearance, accessibility review, volume normalization and testing with users. Functional notifications must remain understandable when sound is muted.

## 20 | Website and marketing applications
### Website structure
Recommended sections: clear promise, current capabilities, language coverage, representative samples, workflow walkthrough, developer entry point, organizational controls, pricing when ready and a final call to action. Show useful evidence before aspirational claims. Use original screenshots with accurate states; mark concept interfaces as illustrative.
### Landing pages
Lead with one user problem and one primary action. Name the supported task and language explicitly. Keep benefits tied to a released feature. Use navy headlines, neutral backgrounds and deep-teal CTAs. Keep contact, pricing and data statements consistent with the actual business. A language-specific page should include reviewed copy and a genuine sample, not a generic translation pasted across many pages.
### Advertising
Use one clear headline, one relevant visual, a readable logo and one action. Maintain logo clear space. Caption audio-led ads. Mark synthetic voice samples where required by product policy or channel context. Do not imply that a celebrity, institution or language community endorses Lugemi. Campaign approval must include actual destination page, offer validity and capability checks.

Publish useful language demonstrations, practical workflow education, documented product updates, responsibly attributed research and collaborator stories with consent. Do not invent posting targets or performance benchmarks without a marketing plan.
### Email and business materials
Use a compact logo, a clear subject, a short introduction and one primary action. Keep essential copy as live text. Signature fields: name, role, Lugemi, verified website and verified contact. Use simple A4 document margins and a small footer with version/date.

## 22 | Print, merchandise and physical use
### Print production
Use vector artwork for signage, stationery and large-format applications. Digital HEX values are the source standard; do not assume a mathematically converted CMYK value is a final print match. Starting conversions: navy C79 M51 Y0 K70; teal C100 M0 Y5 K28; deep teal C100 M0 Y3 K51. These are approximate process conversions, not specified ink recipes or Pantone matches. These values vary with profile, stock and printing method. Obtain a physical proof and confirm legibility before ordering.
### Materials and finishing
Prefer quiet, high-contrast placement on white, navy or neutral materials. Use one-colour marks for embroidery, engraving and limited-colour printing. Simplify tiny details only through an approved optical master. Keep the L open and readable. Do not rely on glows, fine gradients or transparency for production.
### Supplier handoff
Supply the correct vector master, colour specifications, finished size, clear-space requirement and production method. Outline wordmark paths in supplier exports while retaining an editable source internally. Provide bleeds, trim marks and colour profiles according to the printer's requirements. Pantone matches are not specified here; choose them from physical swatches with a print specialist when needed.

## 23 | Responsible brand expression
### Voice identity and permissions
Voice cloning and speaker likeness require documented authorization appropriate to the intended use. Marketing must describe the actual controls, not imply permissions have been obtained universally. Generated speech should be identified where it could be mistaken for a real person's statement. Establish a way to report misuse and correct misleading public content before promoting these features.
### Claims and evidence
Availability, accuracy, latency, security, residency and compliance claims require an owner, evidence and a review date. Never add SOC 2, GDPR compliant or similar badges because they are common in enterprise marketing. Explain controls and verified certifications precisely. This is a brand communications rule; implementation and legal review remain separate obligations.
### Data and language partnerships
Communicate how recordings, text and contributed data are used through actual product notices and agreements. Do not claim that no data is retained or training is opt-in unless the implementation enforces it. Contributor stories should accurately describe consent, attribution, compensation and review. Preserve the distinction between a model's output and a speaker's cultural or linguistic authority.

## 25 | Asset production and governance
### Recommended asset set
After approving a vector master, produce horizontal and stacked SVG/PDF variants in full colour, reversed colour, black and white; transparent PNG variants; symbol-only SVG/PNG; tested favicon sizes; store-specific icons; document and social templates. No SVG, EPS, font binaries, app-store exports or trademark search is supplied by this guide. Generated previews must not be passed off as these production files.
### Naming convention
Use lugemi-logo-horizontal-color-rgb-v1.svg, lugemi-logo-horizontal-white-rgb-v1.svg and lugemi-symbol-teal-rgb-v1.svg as future naming patterns. Include layout, colour, colour space and version. Keep editable masters separate from distribution exports. Do not distribute files labelled final-final. Archive superseded versions with dates and prohibit their use in new materials.
### Ownership and change control
Proposed accountable owner: founder or designated brand lead. Design maintains assets; product owns interface usage; engineering owns token implementation; marketing owns channel templates; language reviewers own approved localized copy. Record an asset owner, version, approval date, licence and intended use. Major name/logo changes increment the major version; colour or layout changes increment the minor version; corrections increment the patch version.

## 26 | Launch checklist and rollout
### Before public launch
Confirm name and domain selection through live registrar checks and appropriate trademark review. Select one logo master and resolve letterform consistency. Create and verify vector exports. Approve exact colour/font standards. Audit actual language and feature coverage. Review the landing page, pricing, data notices and synthetic-content disclosures. Establish real contact information and an asset owner.
### Visual and content QA
Check clear space, minimum size, light/dark contrast and mobile crops. Test real multilingual content, diacritics, RTL and missing glyphs. Verify responsive reflow, keyboard focus, captions and reduced motion. Confirm that claimed capabilities are selectable and working. Review links, CTAs, sample labels and partner permissions. Check raster transparency on both white and navy before shipping.
### Rollout order
First approve the master identity and tokens. Next apply the standards to the console, website and documentation. Then update social accounts, email and business materials. Finally prepare paid campaigns and physical production. Track completion by surface and owner. Measure task completion, readability, brand recognition and conversion using an agreed baseline; do not use this document as proof that the rebrand improves sales.

## 28 | Global market and cultural system
### Africa first: the meaning
Africa first is a sustained product, partnership and evaluation priority. It guides investment in African languages, scripts, dialects, accents, code-switching and local context. It does not exclude other communities or make Africa a single culture. The visual identity remains one global Lugemi system; do not create separate continental logos or stereotyped palettes.
### Regional expression
| Market | Brand emphasis | Localization requirements |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Africa | Language depth, contemporary life and community participation | Region-specific language names, tonal orthography, local varieties, Arabic and African scripts, code-switching |
| Latin America | Regional Spanish and Portuguese, alongside Indigenous and other local languages | Locale-specific vocabulary, pronunciation, names, dates and currencies; no Spain-as-default assumption |
| Southeast Asia | Linguistic and script diversity across individual countries and communities | Script shaping, segmentation, line breaking, diacritics and reviewed local terminology |
| Caribbean | Multilingual communities, creoles and regional varieties | Distinguish languages from accents; respect local orthography and community naming |
| Russia | Russian and other languages used by relevant communities | Cyrillic support and local conventions; never conflate Russian with Ukrainian |
| Ukraine | Ukrainian identity and multilingual user needs | Ukrainian orthography, names, pronunciation and locally reviewed copy |
| India | Many languages and scripts, with varied regional contexts | Script-specific fonts, transliteration choices, code-switching and locale formats |
| Pakistan | Urdu and regional languages with their own identities | Appropriate Urdu typography, RTL and mixed-direction testing; regional-language review |
### Language selectors and coverage wording
Offer search by reviewed language name, local name and code. Let users select a regional variety only when the task supports it. Keep interface language, output language, voice accent, market and data region as separate concepts. Use English labels as a fallback only where the localized label has not been reviewed. Prefer “Languages and regions” to “African versus foreign languages.” Never group Russian and Ukrainian into one language choice.
### Shared identity, local expression
Keep the logo, semantic colours, type hierarchy and component behavior consistent across locales. Adapt copy, fonts, images, examples and formats to the actual audience. Local campaigns should include native-language review and appropriate rights for pictured people and audio. Avoid flags as the default language shorthand, generic ethnic costumes, decorative sacred symbols and cultural motifs without specific context.

## 29 | Application to the current UI and repository
### Preserve the information architecture, refine the brand
The supplied long-form homepage presents a large language intelligence console spanning voice, translation, dubbing, agents, developer tools and organizational workflows. Keep that breadth visible through useful groups and progressive disclosure. The brand should feel welcoming on the website and precise in the console. A new name and palette should not hide existing capabilities or turn every tool into a separate sub-brand.
### Homepage direction
Replace the visible Lugemi identity with Lugemi and lead with “Global language intelligence. Africa first.” Follow with a short speech, translation and language-workflow explanation, a primary “Explore Lugemi” action and a secondary “View language coverage” action. Retain useful product sections from the supplied reference, but reduce repeated card styling and give each section one clear purpose. Use white and mist for the main page, navy for deliberate emphasis, and deep teal for action buttons. Add global-market context after the Africa-first story, with capability links rather than blanket language claims.
### Console direction
Use a clear branded header, workspace selector and account controls. Group tools by Voice and audio, Translation and localization, Knowledge and agents, Developer tools, and Workspace administration. Keep frequent tasks immediately accessible and provide a searchable tool directory for the larger catalog. Show the active tool, project context and task state. Dense workspaces may use tables and split panels; maintain 16px task copy and readable labels. Avoid fitting every route into the top navigation.
### Workflow screens
Speech: keep voice, language, script, consent status, estimated usage and generate action together. Translation: distinguish source and target, glossary, review and export. Dubbing: prioritize timeline, speaker mapping, original audio and review. Knowledge and agents: show sources, execution state and review controls. Developer tools: use real examples, copy controls, authentication guidance and predictable errors. Billing: state usage units, period and charges in plain language. These are design rules; surface only fields and capabilities actually available.
### Repository-grounded claim boundaries
The reviewed core seed registry has 29 languages, including 15 strategic African entries. Vendor-tier seeds include Spanish, Portuguese, Russian, Hindi and Indonesian. Ukrainian and Urdu are absent from that core registry. Ten country packs are African; the five locale seeds do not establish complete global localization. Separate African catalogs cover broader research scope and must not be marketed as released gateway support. The committed evaluation covers three English-to-African-language pairs with twelve segments each and uses fixture evidence. It does not certify live provider quality, every language, or every accent. These findings refer to source at commit 75d22e58097b9971b0bc47785bc7207322d493c8, not a live production audit.
### Rebrand handoff boundaries
Visible identity, metadata, public copy, documentation examples and templates should use Lugemi. Existing package names, API headers, environment variables, stored keys and internal identifiers need a separate compatibility plan before renaming. Historical documentation can retain its historical name. Apply the guide through a reviewed design handoff before publishing code or deploying changes. This document is the brand deliverable; it does not authorize a release.
